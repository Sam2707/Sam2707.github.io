01 · The user

State regulators (who work for the government)

They check applications from people who want to work in finance (e.g. financial advisors), then approve or reject them.

02 · The problem

One case. Information everywhere.

Regulators jumped across 5–7 pages and external documents just to understand one application.

Redesigned the end-to-end workflow (0–1)

One decision. One workspace.

Core insight

The problem wasn't how much data existed. It was how poorly that data was organized around a decision.

It was like keeping the spices in the bedroom and the vegetables in the garage. Everything was technically available, just never where the cooking happened.

04. Diagnosing the legacy experience

Three screens. Three different kinds of friction.

Instead of treating the legacy flow as one vague usability problem, I separated navigation friction, missing decision context, and gaps in the audit trail.

Step 1: Entry

Familiar for experts, difficult for new users

  • Experienced regulators relied on memory to navigate.
  • High-risk queues were easy to miss.
  • New regulators needed time to learn the layout.
The strategic decision to descope
  • Most users spent only 6–8 seconds here
  • High engineering risk (downstream dependencies)
  • Focused on higher-impact parts of the workflow
Step 2: Task list

Task list showed the task, but not the context

  • Critical context missing
  • Manual cross-checking required
  • No inline assignment of task
  • Forced users to the profile details page
Why it broke
  • Key risk signals were separated from the task.
  • Task assignment created a separate workflow break.
Step 3: Task details

It recorded the outcome, but not the reasoning

  • The system captured status changes.
  • Comments and rationale lived in spreadsheets, documents or email.
  • Important context could be lost between regulators.
Why it broke
  • ‘What’ changed was stored, but ‘why’ it changed was not.
  • “Source of Truth” lived outside the secure environment (separate spreadsheets & docs)

06. Iteration and trade-offs

Keep expert speed. Add novice confidence.

An earlier iteration split task information into two rows. Veteran regulators lost their familiar left-to-right scan, while newer users found the dense context and error states unclear. Eye-tracking told us why.

Attention patterns

Same screen. Two different ways of looking for an answer.

Newer regulators searched broadly for orientation. Veterans focused on a smaller set of decision-critical fields. The design needed to support both without making either group slow down.

What we learned

The information was there. Scanning it was hard.

07. Final experience · Session timeouts

Preventing surprise session timeouts.

The redesign brought each decision into one task view. Keeping regulators in that view meant designing the whole workflow around it, including the moment a session is about to end. FINRA’s 30-minute inactivity rule stayed exactly as it was; what changed was what the interface told regulators before it applied.

Before · the existing timeout

No warning before the logout

Idle sessions already ended at 30 minutes, with no sign of the time left. Regulators came back from a PDF or an email to a login screen and MFA.

The real gap

The rule was in place. Timely feedback wasn’t.

Engineering constraints

What keeps a session active, and what doesn’t

Keeps the session active

  • Another tab, same app
  • Continue sessionOnce the server confirms

Doesn’t count; the clock keeps running

  • Opening or reading a PDF
  • Another FINRA appSame login
  • Same app, different login
  • Email or other appsOutside FINRA
After · in-app warning

One warning, three minutes out

At three minutes left, a modal explains why, counts down, and offers Continue session or Log out now.

Optional opt-in

The desktop notification checkbox. Implementation status unverified.

Desktop notification

A nudge at three minutes, and again at one

For regulators working in another app, a desktop notification appears at 3 minutes and again at 1 minute remaining.

Browser support

We started by making sure it worked in Chrome, Safari and Internet Explorer, the browsers about 90% of regulators used.

Failure and expiry states

No renewal until the server confirms

Every path waits for the server. A failed renewal can be retried while time remains; a late one routes to sign-in.

09. Reflection

The biggest win wasn't showing more information. It was making the right evidence impossible to miss.

Enterprise modernization is not a visual-cleanup exercise. It is a prioritization problem: understand where decisions break down, choose the highest-leverage surface, and connect every change to an observable outcome.

01

Auditability is UX

Capture rationale where the work actually happens.

02

Experts and novices coexist

Preserve scan speed; layer guidance on top.

03

De-scoping is design work

The right “not now” protected the outcome.
