Design system migration

Migrating FINRA's design system
from Adobe XD to Figma.

With an unfunded, volunteer team, we moved FINRA UI from Adobe XD to Figma, rebuilt it as a token-based atomic system, and documented it on a live guidebook site.

01. Context & challenge

A fragile library, a volunteer team, no budget

FINRA UI is the shared hub of components and guidelines for FINRA's designers, PMs and engineers. In Adobe XD it had broken links, inconsistent handoffs and no written accessibility rules.

01 · The library

Collaboration bottlenecks

  • Broken links and outdated libraries
  • Inefficient handoffs to engineering
  • Undocumented accessibility standards (WCAG 2.1Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1: the standard for making web content usable by people with disabilities.)
02 · The team

Mixed Figma experience

  • Designers knew Figma to very different degrees
  • The migration had to double as a shared learning experience
03 · The budget

No dedicated funding

  • A non-funded, volunteer initiative
  • Every hour had to count, so the plan had to stay useful at every stage

02. Approach

Rebuild from the smallest parts up

Instead of copying XD files, we adapted Brad Frost's atomic design, studied how Google, Apple and Atlassian organise their systems, and rebuilt in phases.

Atomic design, adapted. Atoms, molecules and organisms live in the system. Templates and pages stay in each product. Source: Brad Frost, Atomic Design.

04. The system

A guidebook site for every decision

Each page shows when to use a component, its states, accessibility checks and code, so design and engineering work from the same rules.

Home

One front door for the whole system

Foundations, components and guidelines in one place, with search.

Color

Eight colour families, mapped to roles

The FINRA palette in ten steps (Blue 70, Slate 30…), semantic tokens for each job, and a WCAG check for every pairing.

Spacing

A named 4px spacing scale

Every token resolves to rem and px, has a typical role, and copies as a CSS custom property.

Buttons

Components you can try before you use them

A live playground for every variant and state, backed by a token table that traces each value to its source.

Inputs

One state contract for every control

Text fields, checkboxes and radios share the same default, active, filled, error and disabled states as the Figma library.

Display modes

Four display modes, designed in Figma

Light, dark, high contrast and paper. Testing with product and engineering teams is what added dark and high contrast.
